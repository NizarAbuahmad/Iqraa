## 2. What December looks like for a Jordanian teacher (the calendar we post against)

| Date | What happens | What it means for content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–14 Dec | Last teaching weeks, revision | Review sheets, short quizzes, mock exams → **"revise without the late nights"** |
| ~13 Dec | Many private schools start finals (e.g. 13–24 Dec) | Hit private-school teachers a few days earlier than public |
| **15 Dec – 4 Jan** | **Ministry first-semester finals, Grades 4–12** (2026/27 calendar) | Marking, results, teacher fatigue → **"get your evenings back"** |
| **18 Dec** | **World Arabic Language Day** (UNESCO theme 2026: «العربية والتنوع الثقافي») | Our pillar «عربي أولًا» + the spelling game. Biggest organic moment of the month. |
| Dec–Jan | Rain and snow days; directorates can delay or close schools | Reactive post ready: free games for kids at home |
| 25 Dec | Christmas, a public holiday in Jordan | Greeting, no selling |
| 31 Dec / 1 Jan | New Year | "2026 in Iqrra" recap + "teacher resolutions" |
| 5–23 Jan | Winter break | — |
| **24 Jan 2027** | **Semester 2 starts** (earlier than the Feb date the master plan assumed) | December ends by pointing at it: «الفصل الثاني نحضّره معك». Move the public launch to the week of 24 Jan. |

Sources: Ministry 2026/27 school calendar (Petra / Roya, 30 Jun 2026); UNESCO World Arabic
Language Day 2026 listing; Petra on directorates setting school hours in bad weather (Dec 2025).

## 3. Trends we ride (and how)

1. **AI in Jordanian education is in the news** — Ministry's Ajyal platform and its smart assistant,
   the Jordan–Syria MoU on AI in education (10 Nov), the World Bank grant for an EdTech & AI
   centre, the 2026–2030 strategy centring teacher development. → Our angle is never "AI";
   it is **"knows your book"**: «مش أي ذكاء اصطناعي — واحد بعرف كتابك». One LinkedIn post a week
   rides the news.
2. **"Prep with me" / POV screen-reels** (silent, captioned, 15–30 s) keep working for teacher
   creators. Every reel is a real screen recording with burned-in Arabic captions; first 2 seconds
   state the pain, not the product.
3. **Year-end "Wrapped" recaps** (Spotify-style) dominate the last week of December → «إقرأ في 2026».
4. **Interactive Stories** (polls, quiz stickers) — the format Arabic education accounts use most
   for engagement → daily story quiz from the spelling and governorates games.
5. **Save-and-share carousels** for teachers (checklists, "mistakes to avoid") — the post type
   teachers save and forward to WhatsApp groups, which is where this market really shares.

## 7. Rules for every post (from the master plan, still binding)

- Brand is **«إقرأ»** and **Iqrra** — never «اقرأ» as the name, never "Iqraa"/"Iqra". Hashtag
  `#إقرأ_للمعلمين`, never bare `#اقرأ`.
- Everything on screen is the real app. A demo label that appears is left visible, never cropped.
- Name the grade/subject/lesson on screen when a reel shows generated material.
- Before posting a reel that shows questions, read every question and its answer key.
- No users, schools, hours-saved or "approved by the Ministry" claims — there is no such approval.
- Arabic is rendered by a browser (all images here are Chrome screenshots), never by a video tool's text renderer.
- Reply to every comment within 24 h; a teacher who reports a mistake gets thanks and «بلّغ عن مشكلة» explained.
